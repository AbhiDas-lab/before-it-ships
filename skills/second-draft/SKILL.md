# Second draft

Claude turns a two-hour job into a five-minute one. This skill is the minutes after: the draft gets questioned before anyone outside the conversation reads it. You are the colleague who reads it first, not the one who signs it. The person who asked signs it.

Never call a draft "ready", "verified" or "checked". You can say where something is and what looks wrong. Only the person can confirm it, by opening the source.

## 1. Settle what it is

From the conversation and the files attached, work out three things. Ask only for what you cannot find, two questions at most, in one message:

- **Who reads it.** A named customer, a department head, a specialist reviewer, finance, a partner, investors, the team, the public.
- **What they should do after reading it.**
- **Whether it leaves the organisation.**

Then set the minimum time the person should spend, and say it once:

| Work | At least |
| --- | --- |
| Quotes and proposals | 30 min |
| Clinical, legal, scientific or safety content | 30 min, and someone qualified reads it |
| Investor, board or partner material | 30 min |
| Customer and partner emails | 15 min |
| Management reports and internal analysis | 15 min |
| Code and website changes | Until it is tested and someone else has looked |
| Notes to yourself | 5 min |

## 2. Trace every number

List every number, date, price, quantity, specification, name and factual claim in the draft. For each one, give the exact spot it came from in the material the person supplied: file name, then line, page or cell, and quote the source text in a few words.

| In the draft | Source | Exact spot | Source says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Rules for this table:

- Only give a location you can actually point to in the supplied files. Never write a plausible page or line. A made-up location is worse than none, because it looks checked.
- If a value is not in the supplied material, write **Not in your files** in the Source column. Say so plainly; it usually means it was filled in from general knowledge or guessed.
- If the source has the number but it means something different (another unit, period, sample, product version or customer), write **Different meaning** and say what differs.
- Sort the table so **Not in your files** and **Different meaning** rows come first.

## 3. Question the draft

Answer each briefly, as plain lists:

1. **What did the draft assume that the person never said?** About the reader, the deal, the product, the timeline.
2. **Read it as the reader named in step 1.** What would they push back on, misread or ask next? Three points at most.
3. **Cut it by a third without losing a fact.** Name the sentences or paragraphs that repeat, pad or say nothing. Do not rewrite yet.

## 4. The organisation's rules

Look for the organisation's own rules first: a house style guide, brand guide, project instructions, a `CLAUDE.md`, or rules the person states. Where they exist, they win. Where they do not, apply the defaults below. Flag only what breaks a rule, and quote the exact sentence.

- **Names spelled as their owners spell them.** Partner, product and customer names keep their registered casing and trademark symbols. If the house rules list correct forms, check every occurrence against that list.
- **The relationship is described as it is.** If the organisation is a reseller, a partner, a distributor or a licensee, the draft uses the term the organisation has agreed with that partner, not a near synonym.
- **No regulated claim that has not been checked** against the owner's current documentation: clinical, diagnostic, regulatory, financial, safety or performance. Where a product's permitted use is restricted (research use only, not for sale in a region, prescription only), the draft claims nothing beyond it.
- **Prices:** none in public content, and none meant for one account in a document for another.
- **Named customers, patients or individuals** appear in external material only with their consent on the exact wording.
- **Writing mechanics** come from the house style. Common ones worth checking even without a guide: one spelling convention throughout, consistent time and date formats, no engagement bait, no inspirational closing line.
- **Look:** anything going outside the organisation uses its design system or brand template if it has one. Partner logos keep their own styling.

## 5. Hand it back

End with this checklist, in this order. After it, add only the one-line offers described below and in section 6:

**Before you send it** (the person does these, not you):
- Open each source at the spot in the table and confirm the value is there and means the same thing. List the rows that need it.
- Resolve every **Not in your files** row: find a source or delete the claim.
- Open the actual file (Word, Excel, PDF, slides), not the preview in the chat.
- Read the first two lines out loud. If they don't sound like a person, rewrite them.
- Minimum time for this kind of work: the figure from step 1.

Then offer once to produce a revised draft that applies the cuts and fixes. When you revise, list what changed in one line each.

## 6. Keep what was learned

When the person has corrected the draft during the conversation, offer once at the end to write those corrections as short instructions they can paste into their project instructions for this kind of work, so the next first draft starts closer.

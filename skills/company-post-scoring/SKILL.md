# Company page: post scoring

Use this for posts on an organisation's company page. Not for posts on a named person's own profile: a person posting under their own name earns attention by sounding like a real person noticing something, which needs a different rubric.

When asked to score a draft, run all three steps and report in the output format below. When asked to draft a post, write it in the page voice, then score your own draft the same way before returning it, and say plainly that a person still needs to score it independently.

## House rules come first

If the organisation has a house style, brand guide, project instructions or a `CLAUDE.md`, read it before scoring. It supplies three things this rubric leaves open:

- **Correct forms of names**: the organisation's own name, its products, its partners, with their casing and trademark symbols.
- **Mechanics**: spelling convention, time and date format, hashtag policy. Where the house rules differ from the defaults in Step 1, the house rules win.
- **What counts as a regulated claim** in its field: clinical, diagnostic, financial, safety, environmental, performance.

If there is none, use the defaults below and say in the output that you did.

## Page voice

- Plain, informative, unhurried. The page is not a person.
- No first-person singular. "We" for the organisation is fine.
- No scenes, no personality, no feelings.
- State what happened, state why it matters to the reader, stop.
- Shape: open concrete, hold the point back a line or two, say it once, stop.
- Paragraphs of one or two sentences. Most readers are on a phone.
- Do not water down technical content to widen the audience.

Bad: "We were blown away by the energy in the room this week!"

Good: "Northfield Analytical Services added a second ICP-MS to its Leeds laboratory in July. Trace-metal results for drinking water now report in four working days rather than ten."

## Step 1: Gate (pass or fail)

The post fails outright if any of these is true. List every failure found, not only the first.

Mechanics (defaults; the house rules may replace them):
- A long dash or a double dash.
- Any emoji.
- Engagement bait anywhere ("thoughts?", "agree?", "let us know in the comments", "we'd love to hear your perspective").
- A question or call to action in the last line.
- A time not in the house format (default: 24-hour clock, such as 20h30, not 8:30pm).
- More than three hashtags, or hashtags that are not real categories.
- A tag on anyone not in the post.
- "I", "me" or "my" anywhere.

Substance:
- A number without a known source.
- A regulated claim, performance claim or specification not checked against the owner's current documentation. If you cannot confirm it was checked, flag it as unverified.
- A named customer, site, individual or account without their consent on the exact wording. If consent is not stated, flag it.
- Any pricing.
- Anything that could be read as a claim the organisation is not permitted to make (a diagnostic claim for a research-use product, a return on a financial product, a certification it does not hold).
- A partner or product name spelled or capitalised wrongly. Check against the house list of correct forms. Without one, check against the owner's own website, and flag any name you could not confirm.

Polls also fail if: the body signals a preferred answer; the body names an option that maps to something the organisation sells; options overlap or miss a real answer; there are more than four options; no one is named to write the follow-up post with a date.

## Step 2: Eight criteria (0, 1 or 2 each, max 16)

When unsure between two scores, give the lower one.

1. **Opens concrete.** 0 premise or definition; 1 real event but no date, place or number; 2 specific event, object, date or number in line one.
2. **Says what happened.** 0 unclear; 1 one of who, what, where, when missing; 2 all four clear and checkable.
3. **Why it matters to the reader, said once.** 0 missing or about the organisation only; 1 implied, or said more than once; 2 plainly says what changes for a customer, user or practitioner, once.
4. **Specifics, not adjectives.** 0 only adjectives (advanced, cutting-edge); 1 one concrete detail; 2 checkable details (product, model, site, date).
5. **No performance.** 0 feeling or hype words (thrilled, excited, blown away, game-changing); 1 one soft word (proud, pleased); 2 none.
6. **Depth fits the audience.** 0 watered down for specialists, or jargon for general readers; 1 pitched at neither; 2 right for the declared audience.
7. **Reads on a phone.** 0 paragraphs of four or more sentences, or a sentence that exists only to sound impressive; 1 one long paragraph; 2 one or two sentences per paragraph, every sentence does work. Read the first two lines as if aloud: would a person say them?
8. **Stops.** 0 lesson, takeaway, "onwards" or summary line; 1 soft flourish or two endings; 2 ends on the last concrete fact.

## Step 3: Threshold and audience

- Declare the audience first: specialists in the field, or broader readers (buyers, management, the wider industry). If the user has not said, state your assumption.
- Publish at 13 or above, with no zero on criteria 2, 3 or 5.
- If the post needs reach, a person should post it first and the page follow.

## Common slips to check

Page talks like a person; opens on a premise; ends on a lesson, summary or question; makes the post about the organisation rather than the reader; restates the point at the end; adjectives in place of facts; waters down a technical post; copies a partner claim without checking; names a customer before consent.

## Output format

1. **Audience**: one line.
2. **House rules**: which source you applied, or "defaults".
3. **Gate**: PASS or FAIL, with each failure quoted from the draft.
4. **Scores**: a table of the eight criteria with score and a one-line reason each.
5. **Total** out of 16 and verdict: publish, or revise.
6. **Fixes**: the specific lines to change, lowest-scoring criteria first. Offer a revised draft only if asked or if the verdict is revise.

Do not use long dashes or emojis in your own output either.
